# G13: the sign of the 101's rail-to-pitch coupling

claim-038 (A101), closing the item HW left open in claim-052.

**Status: DERIVED, not measured.** Instruments: the SH-101 service manual scan
(Nov 1st 1982, circuit diagram, PDF p.9 of
`SH101-reference/Roland-SH101-Service-Manual.pdf`, rendered at native 7340x3490
and cropped) and the genuine mask ROM `SH101-reference/80C49_SH101.bin`, read as
bytes. No hardware was touched and nothing here is a measurement. The
experiment that would make it one is at the bottom.

## The question

HW, claim-052: *"A falling reference through an inverting summer reads FLAT and
you measured SHARP. Until the sign is explained, no rail model is trustworthy,
including this one."*

## The answer: one instruction. `dac_out` writes the DAC code inverted

`SH101.asm:1366`, and verified in the ROM bytes rather than in the
disassembler's opinion:

    dac_out:  cpl  a              ; 0x37
              orl  a,#0c0h        ; 0x43 0xC0   route 11 = not connected
              outl bus,a          ; 0x02
              ret                 ; 0x83

    $ found at 0x3FA, exactly one occurrence in 2048 bytes

So the byte on DB0-5 is `~c`, not `c`. **Every DAC write in the machine goes
through this one routine** (single occurrence; the three call sites at `0x42`,
`0x4a`, `0x96` differ only in which mux channel they then select).

SuperOS reproduces it deliberately, `src/hw.h:119`:

    uint8_t data = (uint8_t)~value;   // active-low data on DB0-5

so this is a property of the instrument, not of either firmware.

## The chain, read off the scan

**The bus byte is one word doing two jobs.** DB0-5 are the DAC, DB6-7 are the
4052's address. From the ROM, with `dac_out` leaving both address bits set:

| site | mask | channel | destination |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0x42`, `0x9f` | `anl a,#3fh` | 0 | **CV OUT (the pitch CV)** |
| `0x4a`, `0x8f` | `anl a,#7fh` | 1 | VCO RANGE |
| `0x96` | `anl a,#0bfh` | 2 | random waveform out |
| `dac_out` | `orl a,#0c0h` | 3 | parked, not routed |

**There is one DAC and it is time-multiplexed into three sample-and-hold caps.**
IC10 (4052) takes the DAC at pin 3 (Y COM) through R72 220; the X bank (pins
11-15) is grounded and unused; C28, C29, C30 are all 0.015 uF, each buffered
(11a non-inverting gain 2 into VR5 "RANGE WIDTH", 11b unity, 9b unity into
R73 1k). The pitch CV is a **sampled** voltage, not a continuous one. This is
the same S/H machinery the `8c0a865` refresh interlock was about.

**The ladder.** R70 30.1k (+VR3 2kB "D/A LINEAR"), R57 62.5k, R58 125k,
R59 250k, R60 500k, R61 1M, summed at the inverting input of 9a, feedback
R71 16.9k || C65 47p. Six bits, matching `cv = note - 36` clamped 0..63 in
`CLAUDE.md` exactly.

**Three independent checks that this reading is right**, none of which I set out
to find:

1. **Scale.** LSB step = R71/R61 * V_rail = 16.9k/1M * 5 V = **84.5 mV**.
   1 V/oct is 83.33 mV. Agreement to 1.4%, and VR3 trims the MSB
   (30.1k + 0..2k, nominal 31.25k = exactly 32x the 1M LSB) which is why that
   trim is called **D/A LINEAR**.
2. **Offset.** R62 15.4k + VR2 1kB inject a fixed current at the same summing
   node. From the -5 V rail that is 5/15.4k * 16.9k = **5.49 V**, against a
   full-scale ladder swing of 63 * 84.5 mV = **5.32 V**. It is the pedestal that
   puts the inverted, negative-going ladder term back into a positive span, and
   it is why that trim is called **D/A TUNE**.
3. The two trim names only make sense under this reading. A scale trim on the
   MSB and an offset trim at the summing node is what a complemented
   binary-weighted DAC needs, and nothing else in the block wants either.

So:

    V_9a(c) = V_offset(-5 V rail) - (R71/R61) * V_rail * (63 - c)

## The sign falls out without reading the 1982 scan any further

HW could not resolve `9a -> R72 -> 4052 -> RANGE -> IC13` off the scan and
neither can I at that resolution. **It is not needed.** The downstream polarity
is pinned by a fact about the instrument that needs no reading at all:

* **a higher key plays a higher pitch** (known, not read);
* a higher `c` makes `(63 - c)` smaller, so `V_9a` moves **more positive**;
* therefore **more positive at 9a = sharper**, whatever 4052 -> CEM3340 does.

Apply the rail sag to the same expression:

* `V_rail` falls -> the subtracted ladder term shrinks -> `V_9a` moves **more
  positive** -> **SHARP.**

**That is the measured direction.** HW's chain was right in every step except
its first: it assumed code up means ladder current up. `cpl a` reverses it, and
the offset pedestal is what makes a complemented ladder usable in the first
place.

## The law, and it is the useful part

    pitch shift (semitones) = -(63 - c) * dV_rail / V_rail        c = note - 36

Two consequences that were not fitted to anything:

1. **The sensitivity is note-dependent and vanishes at the top of the DAC
   range.** At `c = 63` (note 99) a rail sag produces **exactly zero** pitch
   shift. At `c = 0` (note 36) it produces the maximum. Note 60 (`c = 24`) sits
   at 39/63 = 62% of maximum.
2. **A rail error presents as a keyboard SCALE error, not a tuning offset**: a
   fractional rail error is a fractional error on the ladder term only, so the
   keyboard tilts about the top note. It would be mistaken for a
   cents-per-octave calibration fault.

### It also says the quoted droop is too big

At the G3 note (60, `c = 24`) the law needs `dV/V = 5 cents / (39 * 100 cents)`
= 0.128%, i.e. a **6.4 mV** sag to produce the ~5 cents recorded in `CLAUDE.md`.
The recorded 4 -> 8 MHz droop is 20 mV. **Those are the same number as far as
the instrument is concerned**: the bandgap-against-AVCC read steps ~22 mV near
5 V, so 20 mV is one ADC code and cannot be told from 6.4 mV. The law predicts
the true sag is about **3x smaller** than the figure HW's impedance table is
built on, which moves every source-impedance number in `G13_101_RAIL.md` the
same way.

### One number I cannot reconcile, stated rather than smoothed over

`G13_101_RAIL.md:94` records **~57 mV/semitone** as an A101 measurement. The law
gives `V_rail / (63 - c)` = 5000/39 = **128 mV/semitone** at note 60, and
5000/63 = 79 mV/semitone at the lowest note, which is the largest sensitivity
the mechanism permits at any note. **57 mV/semitone is outside the model's range
everywhere.** Either the figure came from the superseded V_OH mechanism, or it
is a real measurement and this model is wrong. I am not guessing which; the
experiment below settles it, and the "5 cents is 2.85 mV" tolerance that number
feeds should not be quoted until it does.

## The S/H changes what "how long does the emulator hold the rail down" means

HW's closing question was burst length, with a table running to 1754 uF for a
1 ms burst. The multiplexed S/H narrows it, `src/hw.h:118`:

    BUS_PORT = data | (ROUTE_NONE << 6);   // present, disconnected
    delayMicroseconds(SETTLE_LADDER_US);   // 10 us, core busy
    BUS_PORT = (data & 0x3F) | (route << 6);
    IdleWaitUs(SETTLE_ACQUIRE_US);         // 100 us, CORE ASLEEP

The held voltage is the ladder voltage at the instant the mux disconnects, and
**the core is idle-sleeping for the whole 100 us acquisition up to that
instant.** The pitch is therefore sampled at the quietest moment of the loop, by
construction. So:

* the relevant row of HW's table is the **10 us** one (18 uF), not the 1 ms one
  (1754 uF), and the board can carry that;
* what should move the pitch is the **DC rail level during idle-sleep**, which
  differs between 4 and 8 and 16 MHz because idle stops the core and not the
  clock system, not a dynamic burst;
* **so this predicts the shift is a static per-clock-speed detune and NOT a
  wobble.** `CLAUDE.md` lists "that the wobble is dynamic at all" as unverified
  and notes the 16 MHz / 4x-emulator prize rests on it. This says it is not, and
  says why.

## The experiment, which is headless and needs no ADC

The rail measurement's 22 mV resolution is the reason claim-038 has been stuck.
**This test never reads the rail.** It uses the instrument's own pitch, which
the timing bench already resolves far better than 5 cents, and it discriminates
by a shape no common-mode confounder can imitate.

`0x3E <mode> <s>` already switches the core over the wire (`src/boot.cpp:268`,
`src/midi.h:79`): mode 0 = the app's own 4 MHz **as a control rather than a
no-op**, bit0 = 8 MHz, bit3 = 16 MHz, bit2 = idle-sleep every pass. No reflash.

For each of notes **36, 60, 99** hold the note and capture through mode 0, then
mode 1, then mode 8.

| note | c | predicted shift, relative |
|---|---|---|
| 99 | 63 | **0.00, exactly** |
| 60 | 24 | 0.62 |
| 36 | 0 | 1.00 |

**Pass condition: the three shifts are in the ratio 63 : 39 : 0.** Note 99 going
flat or sharp by a resolvable amount refutes the mechanism outright. Anything
that detunes the whole keyboard equally is not this mechanism, and the ratio is
what separates them.

Needs the `101` and `audio` leases. Nothing else, no hands, no programmer.

## What is derived and what is measured

* **Read, and checkable by anyone:** `cpl a` at ROM `0x3FA`; the mux channel
  map; the ladder values; the trim names; `hw.h`'s complement and its
  present-then-connect-then-sleep order.
* **Derived:** the sign, the `(63 - c)` law, the 6.4 mV implication, the
  static-not-dynamic prediction. All of it stands or falls on the experiment
  above.
* **Not addressed:** which physical 4052 Y pin carries which channel. I read pin
  numbers off the scan and did not trust them; the ROM's channel numbers are
  what this document uses and they do not depend on that reading.
