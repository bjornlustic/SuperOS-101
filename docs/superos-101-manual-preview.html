Introduction
1

About superOS-101

superOS-101 is custom firmware for a drop-in replacement of the Roland SH-101's original CPU, a Toshiba TMP80C49. Unlike the TB-303, TR-606, and TR-808, which share the NEC µPD650 and therefore share one replacement board, the SH-101 needs its own CPU board and its own firmware, designed from a clean sheet.

The firmware reproduces the original CPU's electrical behavior exactly: the DAC and multiplexer protocol that generates every control voltage in the synth, the keyboard and panel scan, and the gate, clock-reset, and portamento signals. On top of that foundation it re-implements the SH-101's musical features, keyboard, arpeggiator, and 100-step sequencer, against the fully disassembled original ROM as reference.

The Combined Edition holds two complete operating systems in one chip: superOS-101 and 80C49 mode, an emulation of the original SH-101 CPU running the genuine Roland mask ROM. You can switch between them from the panel at any time. See the Combined Edition chapter.

2

How to Use This Manual

Panel Names

Controls are written as they appear on the panel: the function buttons LOAD PLAY DOWN UP&DOWN UP HOLD KEY TRANSPOSE, the RANGE switch (16′ / 8′ / 4′ / 2′), the TRANSPOSE switch (octave down / middle / up), and the envelope TRIG switch (GATE / GATE+TRIG / LFO). The keyboard has 32 keys, F to high C.

A combination such as HOLD + KEY TRANSPOSE means: hold both controls at the same time.

Note The sequencer and arpeggiator are clocked by the LFO or the EXT CLK jack, exactly as on the original, or by MIDI clock when sync is on. Where this manual says "each clock," it means whichever of these is active.
Playing
1

The Keyboard

The keyboard plays monophonically over its 32-key range, with the pitch CV following the original's confirmed 1 V/octave numeric model, so tuning and scaling match a stock SH-101 exactly.

Note Priority

When more than one key is held, the envelope TRIG switch selects which note sounds, as on the original:

  • GATE or LFO: lowest-note priority. The lowest held key sounds.
  • GATE+TRIG: last-note priority. The most recently pressed key sounds; releasing it falls back to the lowest key still held.

In the LFO position the LFO is restarted on each new key press, so an LFO-triggered envelope always starts its cycle in step with your playing.

2

3

The Arpeggiator

Press UP, DOWN, or UP&DOWN to turn the arpeggiator on in that direction; press the same button again to return to normal keyboard play. Hold any group of keys and the arpeggiator steps through them, one note per clock (the LFO, the EXT CLK input, or MIDI clock when sync is on).

  • UP: lowest to highest, then wraps to the lowest.
  • DOWN: highest to lowest, then wraps to the highest.
  • UP&DOWN: bounces between the lowest and highest held keys.
  • RANDOM: press DOWN and UP&DOWN together (the UP&DOWN LED blinks). A random held key each step, never the same note twice in a row. Press both together again to turn it off.

Octave Range

In any arp mode, hold KEY TRANSPOSE and press UP for one more octave or DOWN for one fewer (1 to 3 octaves). UP climbs through the held keys octave by octave, DOWN starts in the top octave, UP&DOWN bounces across the whole span, and RANDOM picks an octave too. The range is remembered across power-off.

Arpeggio and Hold

Press HOLD while an arpeggio plays and it keeps running after you let go of the keys; a new chord replaces it. A HOLD pedal works the same way (unless it is routed to KEY TRANSPOSE, see below).

4

Key Transpose, Tune and the Pedal

Key Transpose (as the original)

Hold KEY TRANSPOSE and press a key: everything that plays (keyboard, arpeggio and sequence) is transposed by the distance from the lowest C (the 8th key, C4). A key below it transposes down, above it up. The transposition stays after you let go, with the KEY TRANSPOSE LED lit. To return to normal, hold KEY TRANSPOSE and press the lowest C.

Unlike the original, the transposition also reaches the CV OUT jack and MIDI OUT, so external gear follows it.

Transpose Mode (double tap)

Double-tap KEY TRANSPOSE to enter transpose mode (the LED blinks). While the sequencer plays, or while a HOLD-latched arpeggio runs, a plain key press transposes, with no button held: play the key you want the line in. Double-tap again to leave transpose mode; the transposition itself stays until you set it back to the lowest C.

Tune

From normal keyboard play, hold KEY TRANSPOSE and press LOAD. The SH-101 holds an A with the gate open (the LOAD LED blinks): 440 Hz with RANGE at 8′ and TRANSPOSE in the middle, and MIDI OUT sends note 69 at the same time for a tuner. Set the TUNE knob, then press any function button to leave.

The HOLD Pedal as Key Transpose

In the Config Menu, key 19 routes the HOLD pedal jack to act as the KEY TRANSPOSE button: hold the pedal and press a key to transpose, double-tap it for transpose mode, and while recording it enters rests. Key 19 again returns the pedal to HOLD. The choice is remembered.

The Sequencer
1

Recording a Sequence

Press LOAD while stopped to record a new pattern into the current slot (the LOAD LED lights; the slot is cleared). Every key you press is added as the next step, up to 128 steps. The keyboard sounds while you record, and notes arriving over MIDI record too.

ControlWrites
Keyboard keyA note step at that pitch (TRANSPOSE applies as played).
KEY TRANSPOSEA rest step.
Hold HOLD + keyA legato step: the note starts without the gate dropping, so the envelope does not retrigger and, with PORTAMENTO on AUTO, it glides.
UPCycles the last step’s ratchet: 1, 2 or 3 hits inside the step.
DOWNTakes back the last step.
LOAD againEnds recording; the pattern is saved automatically.

Correcting and Adding (as the original)

Press LOAD while the sequence plays and recording continues from the step after the one sounding: the steps you enter replace the old ones from there, and anything after them stays and still plays. Press LOAD again to finish.

Real-Time Recording

While the sequence plays, hold PLAY and press LOAD (the PLAY and LOAD LEDs alternate). Now play along: each key lands on the step it was pressed in, or on the next step once the current one is half over. A key still held at the half-step ties into the next step (legato). KEY TRANSPOSE writes a rest. An empty pattern becomes 16 rests to play into. Hold PLAY and press LOAD again to stop recording; the edits are saved when playback stops.

2

Playing a Sequence

Press PLAY to start the sequencer; press it again to stop. Steps advance one per clock: note steps retrigger the gate at their pitch, rest steps close the gate, legato steps change pitch with the gate held. At the end of the pattern playback wraps around.

Direction

While the sequence plays: UP = forward, DOWN = backward, UP&DOWN = ping-pong, DOWN and UP&DOWN together = random. The direction is remembered across power-off (Config Menu keys 15 to 18 set it too).

Loop Length, Live

While playing, hold the HOLD button and press a key: the sequence loops only its first steps, one step per key counting from the lowest key (the 4th key = 4 steps, the 16th = 16 steps). The pattern itself is untouched. Press the same key again for the full length.

Shift

While playing, hold KEY TRANSPOSE and press UP to play the next step now, or DOWN to play the previous one. This moves the sequence against the clock, to line it up with other gear or for variation.

Nudge

While playing, hold the HOLD button and press UP to move every note one step right (later), or DOWN to move them one step left (earlier). Ratchets move with their notes and the pattern wraps around. Unlike Shift, the timing does not move: the playhead and the clock stay where they are and only the notes change places.

The move lands at the next half-step, after the sounding note has started and before the next note is chosen, so nothing is cut, doubled or retriggered and you never hear it happen. The web editor has the same as ◀ Nudge / Nudge ▶ (Shift + arrow keys), with Undo.

Resync

The editor’s Resync button puts the sequence back on the one: on the host’s beat under MIDI clock, otherwise where it would be counting from PLAY. It also undoes a Shift. Changing direction, loop length, pattern or clock division never moves the sequence off the beat in the first place: its position is always worked out from the steps played since the start. Ping-pong plays its end steps twice, so a round trip stays on the bar.

Note The keyboard transpose (see Key Transpose) applies to the sequence as it plays. The TRANSPOSE switch does not: a pattern keeps the octave it was recorded in, and while it plays the switch picks the performance row (see Performance Modes).
3

Patterns and Songs

There are sixteen pattern slots of up to 128 steps each, and saving is automatic. The sixteen lowest keys (F3 to G#4) are patterns 1 to 16.

ToDo
Hold PLAY, press a key 1…16Stopped: selects that pattern and starts it when you release PLAY. Playing: the new pattern follows at the end of the current pass, so the change lands on the bar.
Hold PLAY, press several keys in orderA song: the patterns play one after another in that order (up to 16 entries, repeats allowed), looping.
Key 25…32 alone while playing, TRANSPOSE on LSwitches to pattern 1…8 now, keeping the position (in sync when the pattern lengths are multiples of each other). With Performance modes off, keys 1…16 do this for patterns 1…16 at any TRANSPOSE setting.
MIDI Program Change 0…15Selects pattern 1…16 (at the end of the pass while playing).
SaveNothing to press: about half a second after an edit (or when playback stops), the pattern is written to permanent memory.
Back up / edit on a computerThe web editor (USB-C or DIN MIDI) reads and writes all sixteen slots, ratchets included, and saves them as a file.

Patterns survive power-off with no battery. Slot selection is ignored while recording, so finish with LOAD first. Firmware updates never touch the pattern memory.

4

Randomize and Scale

Hold PLAY and press UP&DOWN for a new random pattern (rhythm and pitch), UP for new pitches on the same rhythm, or DOWN to mutate it (two to four small changes). It works stopped or playing and never starts play. The editor adds rhythm-only, octave and legato randomizers, a density setting and a repeatable seed.

Scale

Each pattern can have a scale (the 35 scales of the superOS-303). Hold PLAY and KEY TRANSPOSE and press a key: that note becomes the root, in Major; the same key again steps to the next scale. Hold PLAY and tap KEY TRANSPOSE alone to turn the scale on or off. The scale never changes the stored notes: the output snaps each note to the nearest note of the scale (the lower one on a tie), before any transpose. Randomized notes are written in the scale.

5

Performance Modes

While a sequence plays, the 32 keys become a performance surface, laid out as the overlay strip above them. The TRANSPOSE switch picks the row: L = 1 Global, M = 2 Pitch, H = 3 Time. Keys are counted from the lowest F (key 1) to the top C (key 32).

Nothing here is written into the pattern: every change acts on what you hear, and the stored notes stay as recorded. A choice stays on (latched) until you press the same key again, or reset it. The overlay is active only while the sequencer plays and no function button is held, so HOLD + key (loop length), PLAY + key (patterns and songs), KEY TRANSPOSE + key and transpose mode work as before. Stopped, in LOAD, in real-time record and with the arpeggiator, the keys play notes and the TRANSPOSE switch shifts the octave.

Row 1: Global (TRANSPOSE on L)

KeysFunction
1…12Transpose by the distance from the lowest C (key 8 = none), as Key Transpose.
13…19Octave variations 1…7: each step lifts or drops an octave by a stored pattern (2nd steps up, 4th steps up, 2nd steps down, up and down, pairs up, second half up); 7 picks a random octave on every step.
20…24LFO/S&H clock sync 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1/1: the LFO (and the random S&H it clocks) restarts on that grid.
25…32Stored patterns 1…8, switched now at the same position.

Row 2: Pitch (TRANSPOSE on M)

KeysFunction
1…7Reorder notes, rests stay where they are: as written, reverse, cycle even (2nd, 4th … notes first), cycle odd, prime order (a prime stride through the notes), start one forward, start one back.
8…19Root key C…B of the quantizer.
20…26Quantization key type: major, minor, dorian, mixolydian, lydian, phrygian, locrian. A root alone uses major, a type alone uses C. Overrides the pattern's own scale while on.
27…31Combine other sequence: add, subtract, multiply, divide, difference. The intervals of the next pattern (above its first note) are combined with this pattern's (above its first note), step by step, then folded back by octaves into range. A rest in the other pattern leaves the note alone.
32Reset (see below).

Row 3: Time (TRANSPOSE on H)

KeysFunction
1…7Shift time: a quarter note right, left; an eighth right, left; a sixteenth right, left; three sixteenths right. Right = later.
8…12Skip time: skip every 4th, every 8th, even, odd, every 5th clock. A skipped clock is silent and the pattern waits for the next one.
13…17Add / delete time, the grid clock: 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/4 triplets, 1/16 triplets.
18…21Add / delete time, the logic: NOR, OR, AND, XOR. The step clock and the grid clock go through the gate; each rising edge of the result plays a step. OR adds steps on the grid, AND keeps only the steps on it. A grid alone uses OR, a logic alone uses 1/4.
22…26Ratchet, only while held: 1/32, 1/16, 1/16 triplets, 3 and 7 times a step. The note sounding at the press repeats; the pattern runs on underneath and continues in place when you let go.
27…31Combine other sequence: AND, OR, NOR, XOR, NAND of this pattern's rhythm with the next pattern's. A step the logic fills plays the next pattern's note (NOR: the last note).
32Reset (see below).

Reset

In rows 2 and 3 the top key (32) is Reset. Tap it alone to clear the whole row. Hold it and press a key to turn that one function off. Row 1 has no Reset: press the same key again, or key 8 for no transpose.

Note The other sequence is always the next pattern (pattern 16 pairs with pattern 1). Times count one step as a sixteenth. The add / delete grid follows the MIDI clock when it drives the SH-101; otherwise it is worked out from the step clock's own speed, so with a rhythmic EXT CLK it follows that rhythm. LFO/S&H sync with the MIDI clock sets the LFO hard-sync division until you turn it off; without it, the LFO restarts every 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 steps.

To give the keys back their pre-overlay roles while playing (keys 1…16 switch patterns now, the TRANSPOSE switch does nothing to a playing sequence), turn Performance modes off in the web editor's Performance panel. The setting is kept across power-off.

MIDI
1

MIDI Clock Sync

With MIDI clock sync on (Config Menu), incoming MIDI clock becomes the instrument’s clock, divided to the rhythm you choose, and the SH-101 becomes a strict transport slave:

  • Start starts the sequencer, and the first step sounds on the first clock after Start (the downbeat). Stop stops it.
  • Only the incoming clock moves the machine: with no clock arriving it stays silent, and pressing PLAY just arms it to start when the clock arrives.
  • Timing is smoothed the same way as on the superOS-303 and 808 (uClock): each clock byte is placed on a steady timebase, so USB and MIDI delivery jitter does not reach the sound, and no clock is ever dropped or doubled.
  • The LFO can be hard-synced to the clock (Config Menu key 14) and the random S&H steps once per division.

Divisions

Ten divisions, exact at 24 pulses per quarter (no drift): 1/4, 1/4 dotted, 1/4 triplet, 1/8, 1/8 dotted, 1/8 triplet, 1/16, 1/16 dotted, 1/16 triplet, 1/32. Choose them in the Config Menu.

Sync Delay

Config Menu keys 20 to 31 set a sync delay of 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 18, 21, 25 or 30 ms: the whole slaved machine plays that much later than the clock, to line it up with slower gear or a slower listening path. It can only delay (a note cannot play before its clock arrives).

Note A master that compensates latency itself (for example a drum machine that sends clock early and measures its inputs) will cancel a sync delay that makes the SH-101 its latest input; leave the delay at 0 there.
Combined Edition
1

Two Firmwares in One Chip

Like the superOS-303 Combined Edition, one image carries two complete firmwares, with one active per boot:

  • superOS-101: the firmware described in this manual.
  • 80C49 mode: an emulation of the original SH-101 CPU running the genuine Roland mask ROM, for a bone-stock instrument.

The selection is remembered across power-off; the unit always boots into whichever firmware you last chose. Switching is done from the Config Menu and takes effect immediately; USB stays connected.

2

The Config Menu

Hold HOLD + KEY TRANSPOSE for 2 seconds, in either firmware, while playing or idle. You can also hold the combination while switching the power on. In the menu the HOLD LED lights, the gate is forced off, and the function LED shows which firmware is currently running: LOAD lit = 80C49 mode, PLAY lit = superOS-101.

3

80C49 Mode

In 80C49 mode the unit runs the original SH-101 program, byte for byte. The emulated CPU's ports are wired straight through to the synth, so the original's own scan patterns, DAC writes, and timing loops drive the analog board unchanged: every behavior of a 1982 unit is reproduced, including its quirks. For the workflow in this mode, the original Roland SH-101 owner's manual is the manual.

  • The keyboard, arpeggiator, sequencer, hold, and key transpose all behave exactly as stock.
  • The original service Test Mode works: hold LOAD + KEY TRANSPOSE while switching the power on (see the Roland service manual for the test procedures).
  • The superOS Config Menu combo (HOLD + KEY TRANSPOSE, 2 s) still works, so you can always switch back.
  • MIDI clock sync works here too: with sync on, the divided MIDI clock is fed to the original program in place of the LFO / EXT CLK, so even the stock arpeggiator and sequencer run DAW-locked at the chosen division.
  • MIDI notes work here too: notes 53…84 (F3…C6, the SH-101's own keys) are pressed into the emulated key matrix, so the original program sees them as keys and they arpeggiate, record and transpose like keys. Notes outside that range have no key and are ignored. Whatever the original program plays is echoed on MIDI OUT.

Sequence Memory in 80C49 Mode

The original CPU kept its sequence in battery-backed internal RAM. In 80C49 mode that RAM region (the 100 sequencer steps) is mirrored to the CPU's permanent memory: about a second after you finish recording it is saved, and it is restored before the original program starts, so the sequence survives power-off with no battery, exactly as a healthy stock unit would keep it. superOS-101's own sixteen patterns are separate and unaffected.

The Mask ROM

The original ROM is Roland's copyrighted code and is never distributed with superOS-101. It is embedded at build time from your own dump of an SH-101's 2 KB ROM. A build made without a dump still works fully as superOS-101; only 80C49 mode is unavailable.

Note The emulator is paced to the original's 6 MHz clock. On the current CPU board the emulated program may run somewhat below full speed; pitch CVs and clock-driven tempo are unaffected (the arpeggiator and sequencer follow the LFO / EXT CLK), but key-scan response can be slightly slower than a stock unit. This is being measured during board bring-up.
Reference
2

Development Status

Implemented and verified against a pin-level simulation of the synth running the real firmware, with the clock verified on hardware against an MPC Live II:

  • Keyboard, HOLD, key transpose (with transpose mode), tune, arpeggiator with random mode and octave range.
  • Sequencer: 16 patterns, ratchets, directions, live loop length, shift, songs, real-time recording, correcting and adding.
  • MIDI notes in/out, MIDI clock slave with uClock smoothing and sync delay, SysEx editor and updater.
  • 80C49 mode (the original ROM), with MIDI and clock sync.

Not included: the SH-1oh1-style extra filter envelope and filter LFO, which need the VCF/VCA hardware mod. None of the superOS performance features exist in 80C49 mode, which is stock by design.

Specifications

  • CPU AT90USB1286 (run at 4 MHz to keep the pitch rail quiet) on a drop-in board for the SH-101's 40-pin 80C49 socket. USB-C MIDI and DIN MIDI; firmware updates over MIDI SysEx.
  • CV engine Bit-faithful reproduction of the original DAC/multiplexer protocol: 6-bit pitch, range, and random CVs into the original sample/holds, with the original's scan-safety guarantees. Confirmed 1 V/octave numeric model.
  • Keyboard 32 keys, monophonic, lowest- or last-note priority (TRIG-switch selected), octave TRANSPOSE, RANGE 16′–2′.
  • Arpeggiator Up, down, up&down and random over held keys, 1 to 3 octaves; clocked by LFO, EXT CLK or MIDI clock.
  • Sequencer 16 patterns of up to 128 steps of note / rest / legato with 1x/2x/3x ratchets; forward, backward, ping-pong, random; nudge; randomize and 35 scales; songs of up to 16 patterns; step and real-time recording; saved automatically to permanent on-chip memory (no battery).
  • MIDI clock sync 24-ppqn realtime clock, strict transport slave, uClock jitter smoothing, 0 to 60 ms sync delay; ten drift-free divisions (1/4 … 1/32 with dotted and triplet); clocks the arp/sequencer, hard-syncs the analog LFO via CLOCK RESET, and steps the random S&H; works in both firmwares (injected on the emulated T1 in 80C49 mode). Settings retained across power-off.
  • 80C49 mode Full-ISA MCS-48 emulation of the original CPU running the genuine mask ROM (user-supplied, never distributed), ports passed 1:1 to the synth, paced to the original 6 MHz clock.
  • Firmware select Panel Config Menu (HOLD + KEY TRANSPOSE, 2 s or at power-on); choice retained across power-off.
