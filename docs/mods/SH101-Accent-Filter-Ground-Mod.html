SuperOS-101 · hardware install guide

SH-101 accent, filter CV and ground mods

Four wires from the SuperOS-101 CPU board (v1.1 or v1.2, MCP4822 CV DAC) and the synth's own boards, landing on the same points Tubbutec uses for the SH-1oh1: filter CV between the ENV MOD and VCF MOD faders, volume accent on the middle leg of TR28, a +15 V bias wire to C12, and the R63 ground strap to the jack board.

Difficulty: moderate solderingTime: about 1.5 hReversible: yesBoard refs: Roland service manual, Nov 1982

Wire 3 goes to +15 V on every board. Never wire it to −5 V (jack board pin 40, the original v1.1 instructions): the VCF node sits at +5 V, so −5 V through R10 drains it, pins IC18b’s output at +12 V and the synth goes silent except for faint thumps (first install, 2026-09-29).
v1.1 boards (R10 68k, pad labelled -5V): wire that pad to +15 V and keep the 68k. This is the first unit’s configuration, measured 2026-09-30: MIDI cutoff reaches about 0.4 of a FREQ-fader throw down and 1.5 up (CC 0 to about 37 all land on the lower limit). v1.2 boards (R10 100k, pad labelled +15V) reach about 1.1 down and 0.9 up; swapping a v1.1’s R10 to 100k gives the same. Either way, calibrate the center for the R10 you have (section 07).

01Wiring overview

Harness drawing, not to scale. The synth board is hinged: it folds up to expose the control board's foil side underneath.

02Before you start

ToolsTemperature-controlled iron, fine tip (330 to 350 °C), 0.5 mm leaded solder, flux pen, flush cutters, wire strippers, Phillips #1 and #2
Wire26 AWG stranded, PTFE or silicone insulation, in four colours if possible; 20 AWG (or 2 × 24 AWG) for the ground strap
MeterDMM with continuity beeper and a 2 V DC range; this guide relies on it at every joint
AlsoSmall cable ties or Kapton tape, a craft knife to scrape solder mask, a USB-MIDI host for the final setup
  1. Unplug power, MIDI, USB and every jack. Remove the batteries.
  2. Remove every screw on the bottom plate and lift the plate off. The synth board (brown, "SH-101 SYNTH. BOARD" silk) is now facing you.
  3. For the ground mod (R63) and the accent wire (TR28) you can work right here. For the VCF wire, remove the screws holding the synth board, unplug the two connectors on the side away from the keyboard, and fold the board up to expose the green foil side of the control board.
  4. Take a photo of every connector before you unplug it.
Work on the board with the SuperOS CPU board out of its socket while soldering on the synth board near IC6, and put it back before the power-on checks. Keep the iron off the socket itself.

03Ground mod: R63 to the jack board

Standalone mod, works on a stock SH-101 too. Tubbutec measured about 10 dB less digital noise, and it improves pitch CV accuracy because the DAC's return current no longer flows through the thin single-sided ground traces.

  1. Find the ground leg of R63. Power off. Put one meter probe on header pin 42 of jack board A (or any jack sleeve) and touch each leg of R63. The leg that beeps is the ground leg. The other leg reads open (it is a DAC bit line through 1 M).
  2. Cut a ~30 cm length of 20 AWG wire (or twist two 24 AWG wires together, as Tubbutec did). Strip and tin 3 mm at each end.
  3. Solder one end to the ground leg of R63 on the component side, wrapping it once around the lead above the board. Do not touch the other leg or R65 next to it.
  4. Route the wire to jack board A and solder the other end to the ground bus beside the CV IN jack (J6), on the solder side. Tubbutec used the large sleeve lug at the edge of the board. Only the back cover needs to come off to reach both points.
  5. Check: 0 Ω from the R63 joint to header pin 42. No continuity from the wire to the other leg of R63.

04Accent: VCA pad to TR28

  1. Cut ~30 cm of 26 AWG wire (yellow). Strip and tin 2 mm at each end.
  2. Solder one end into the SuperOS VCA pad (middle of the three; the pads are plated through, so either face works). Solder from the top so the wire exits away from the socket.
  3. On the synth board, solder the other end to the middle leg of TR28, on the leg above the board. Tubbutec's note applies: the three legs are close, so a small joint and no bridges to the outer legs.
  4. Check (power off): the wire is not shorted to TR28's outer legs. The outer legs are the grounded base and the emitter.
Do not use a 4.7k or smaller resistor elsewhere to "boost" the accent. The firmware caps the VCA amount at 100 so envelope plus accent stays near the BA662's ~2 mA control-current rating. Leave the cap in place.

05Filter CV: VCF pad to the IC18b node

  1. Fold the synth board up (section 02, step 3). The green foil side of the control board is now visible with the fader legends RESO, ENV MOD, VCF MOD, KEY FOLLOW.
  2. Confirm the pad with the meter: it must beep to pin 6 of IC18 (IR9022, the 8-pin DIP just left of the ENV MOD fader) and to one leg each of R191, R192 and R193.
  3. Cut ~35 cm of 26 AWG wire (red). Solder one end into the SuperOS VCF pad (the pad nearest the pin-1 / USB end of the three).
  4. Tin the pad lightly, then tack the wire to it with a small joint. If you land on the trace instead of the pad, scrape a 2 mm window of solder mask first, as in figure 2 of the Tubbutec manual.
  5. Route the wire along the fold so it does not pinch when the synth board closes. Tape it down near the joint to take strain off the pad.
This node is a virtual ground. It carries only microamps and is sensitive: keep the wire away from the power supply section and the CPU clock area, and never probe it with the power on using anything other than a meter.

06+15 V bias: +15V pad to C12

The pad is labelled +15V on v1.2 boards and -5V on v1.1 boards; on both it goes to +15 V.

  1. Find C12 on the synth board (the same board as the CPU socket, so the synth board does not need folding): the 2.2 µF / 50 V electrolytic between R21/R22 and the white connector, where Tubbutec solders their orange +15 V wire (figure 1 of their manual).
  2. Confirm before soldering: power on, black probe on ground (jack board A header pin 42, or any jack sleeve), red probe on each leg of C12. Use the leg that reads about +15 V; the other (the stripe side) reads 0 V. Write the reading down, and also IC18 pin 5 (about +5 V; socket pin 40 is the same rail): section 07 needs both. Power off again.
  3. Cut ~15 cm of 26 AWG wire (violet or blue). Solder one end into the SuperOS +15V / -5V pad (the outermost of the three, beyond socket pin 20) and the other end to that leg of C12.
  4. Alternative: the +15 V end lug of the FREQ fader VR17 on the control board’s foil side (the end lug that reads +15 V and does not change when the fader moves). It is the same rail, with a longer wire across the hinge.
The +15V pad sits right beside socket pin 20, which is ground. A solder bridge there shorts the +15 V rail. Inspect it with a loupe. Power off, the +15V pad must not read 0 Ω to pin 20.

Firmware

  1. Open the SuperOS menu: hold HOLD + KEY TRANSPOSE for 2 s. Key 11 selects the MC-202 accent model, key 12 the TB-303 model, key 13 toggles MIDI filter cutoff. KEY TRANSPOSE exits.
  2. Accents come from two places: MIDI notes with velocity ≥ 100 (default velThr), and sequencer steps marked accented. On the panel, hold U&D while you enter the note in LOAD (add HOLD for an accented legato step); in the editors, the ACC key on a step. Accented steps go out on MIDI OUT at velocity 127. The accent lasts exactly one note, as on the MC-202: it decays toward the sustain level while the note is held and releases in 7 ms when the next step starts. Cutoff follows CC 74 (14-bit with CC 106 as the LSB), and each sequencer step can carry its own filter CV (the FLT control on a step in the editors; 64 = the slider’s own position, off = follow the CC).
  3. Calibrate the filter CV offset (the center: the DAC level where MIDI cutoff adds nothing). From the section 06 readings, in mV: center = Vpin5 − (V+15 − Vpin5) × 27k / R10. R10 68k (v1.1): 4.87 V and 15.0 V give 848 (the first unit runs 850). R10 100k (v1.2): 5.00 V and 15.0 V give 2300 (the firmware default). Set it in the web editor’s Filter CV · Accent panel (enter the two readings and R10, press Set), on the MPC’s 101 page (Config → Filter CV offset; DATA moves 10 mV, SHIFT 1 mV), or in the Mac editor’s Config. The same places set the accent model and amounts and the MIDI OUT / THRU jack mode.
  4. Check it: with the FREQ slider at half, CC 74 at 64 must sound the same as the slider alone, and toggling MIDI cutoff (menu key 13) must not move IC18 pin 7. CC 0 closes the filter, CC 127 opens it past the slider.
  5. By SysEx, for hosts without an editor: F0 7D 23 F7 reads the config (reply 0x24: center lo7, center hi5, cc, flags, mode, vcfAmount, vcaAmount, decay10ms, velThr, resDepth, buildup, hw, the live codes A and B, then vcaPark lo7, hi5); F0 7D 25 <14 bytes> F7 writes it (the 12 above then vcaPark). Keep hw = 1.
Done when: CC 74 sweeps the cutoff both ways from the FREQ slider’s position, velocity-127 notes and accented steps are louder and brighter than plain ones, and the idle output is quieter than before the ground strap.

Sources. Solder points follow the Tubbutec SH-1oh1 installation manual (red filter wire between ENV MOD and VCF MOD, yellow accent wire on the middle pin of TR28, ground strap from R63 to the jack board), their SH-101 noise reduction mod, and the µTune edition manual. Board artwork is cropped from the Roland SH-101 service manual (Nov 1st 1982), pages 5 to 7. Node identities (IC18b pin 6, TR28 collector to BA662 pin 1, jack board A pin 42 = GND, IC18 on +15 V / GND with pin 5 on the +5 V D/A reference) are read from that schematic. Pad positions and component values come from SuperOS-101CPU/design.py and superos101cpu.kicad_pcb; firmware behaviour from src/cvdac.h and src/menu.h.

Wire lengths are estimates: cut long, route, then trim. Verify every point with a meter before soldering.
