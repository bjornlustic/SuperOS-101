=== WHY NOTE 99 IS READ AT h2 AND NOT AT h16, WHICH IS AN INSTRUMENT DEFECT ===

`pick_harmonic` returns the HIGHEST harmonic under 0.40*rate. At note 99
(nominal 2489.02 Hz, 48 kHz) any request >= 7 clamps to 7, so the published
recipe `--harmonic 16` was measuring h7 at 17423 Hz.

MEASURED HARMONIC ENERGY, one capture, coherent magnitude re fundamental:

  note 36:  h7 -14.1   h8 -19.7   h16 -25.2   h32 -37.4 dB
  note 99:  h2  +0.0   h3 -21.6   h4 -19.0    h7 -47.6 dB   (h16, h32 above Nyquist)

So at note 99 the estimator was reading the WEAKEST partial in the capture,
48 dB down and in the noise, while h2 was the strongest component present.
The consequences, all measured:

  * h7 within-cell sd 1.17 c (mode 0) against h2/h4/h6's 0.05-0.09 c: 13x worse.
  * the h7 shift MOVES with the harmonic (h1 -4.321, h3 -0.945, h5 -0.418,
    h7 -0.152 c) -- estimator-generated by the tool's own criterion.
    h2, h4 and h6 agree to 0.0005 c and give +0.572.
  * the per-capture screen at h7 turned into an ARM-ASYMMETRIC selector:
    33 of 100 mode-0 captures dropped against 3 of 100 mode-1, 4 of the 5
    runs FLAGGED over the 10 % drop limit, and it manufactured +0.65 c out of
    an unscreened -0.05 c. At h2 it drops ZERO of 200 and the answer is stable.
    The screen was not broken; it was correctly reporting that its input was.
  * odd harmonics at note 99 (h3 h5 h7) are weak and biased; even ones
    (h2 h4 h6) are strong and agree. That is a property of this note's
    spectrum, not of the arms.

INDEPENDENT CONFIRMATION, structurally different estimator. Band-pass around
4970 Hz, interpolated rising zero crossings, least-squares period over ~2980
cycles, run n99-r1:

    mode 0  4969.8242 Hz  sd 0.2590      mode 1  4971.5280 Hz  sd 0.2456
    shift +0.5934 c   against the DFT harmonic-referral's +0.5724 c

Two estimators sharing no code path agree to 0.021 c on a 0.6 c effect.

THE DEFECT TO FIX: `pick_harmonic` chooses by Nyquist alone. It should choose
by MEASURED energy in the capture. Note 36 and note 60 are unaffected (their
h16/h32 pair is strong and self-consistent, and that is now shown rather than
assumed by the systematic column above), but the docstring's claim that
"note 99 is the note that needs the help least" is wrong: note 99 is the note
where the recipe silently selects an unusable partial.

=== WHAT A SKEPTICAL READER SHOULD ATTACK ===

1. NOTE 99 IS READ AT A DIFFERENT HARMONIC FROM THE OTHER TWO ROWS. This is
   NOT the harmonic-mixing that produced the withdrawn 5.4 sigma: that mixed
   two harmonics WITHIN one comparison where they disagreed. Here each note's
   reading is verified stable across its own neighbouring harmonics (36/60:
   h16 vs h32; 99: h2 vs h4 vs h6) and the per-note spread is carried as the
   systematic column. But it IS a per-note estimator choice and a reader is
   owed the sentence.
2. THE DOMINANT COMPONENT AT NOTE 99 SITS AT 4970 Hz, i.e. 2x nominal, and
   which physical thing that is (2nd harmonic of the VCO, or the VCO itself
   with the 2485 Hz peak being the sub-oscillator) is UNRESOLVED. It does not
   affect the result, because both arms are read on the same component and
   the measurement is a difference, but it is not known and is stated as such.
   The 2485 Hz region is a doublet (2496.7 and 2473.3 Hz, within 1 dB) which
   is itself unexplained.
3. STILL NO RAIL-VERSUS-LOAD ATTRIBUTION. Modes 4 (idle-sleep) and 8 (16 MHz)
   have never produced a usable cell. Unchanged by this work.
4. THE CONSTANT TERM HAS NO MECHANISM YET. +0.60 c that does not depend on cv
   is a second effect sitting on top of the rail term, and naming it is the
   next job. It is a fit parameter here, not an explanation.
5. chi2 3.33 on 1 dof: note 60 sits +1.6 sd above the affine line. Three
   points and two parameters is a weak test of a two-parameter law. A fourth
   note would test it properly.
