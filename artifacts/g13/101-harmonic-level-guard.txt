claim-038: the harmonic level guard, break-tested
instrument tools/diag/g13_sign.py harmonic_levels(); saved captures, no hardware.

WHY IT EXISTS: pick_harmonic() chooses the highest harmonic under Nyquist and
nothing in the chain ever asked whether that partial had energy in it. At note
99 that selected h7 at -54 dB and produced a confident wrong answer for five
repeats. Now the run REFUSES the note and names the remedy.

L9  (prove the guard FIRES) and L90 (prove the named REMEDY clears it):

  note  requested  selected  level    verdict
    36         16  h16       -20.4 dB  accept
    60         16  h16       -12.7 dB  accept
    99         16  h7        -54.0 dB  REFUSE -> re-run with --harmonic 6
    99          6  h6        -35.0 dB  accept
    99          2  h2         +0.0 dB  accept

  Fires on exactly the case that was wrong, and on nothing else.
  Notes 36 and 60 are ACCEPTED unchanged at h16, so the two published rows
  are not disturbed by this guard: that is the point of showing them here.
  The remedy it prints for note 99 (--harmonic 6) CLEARS it: h6 is -35.0 dB.
  Note 99 was ultimately published at h2 (+0.0 dB), which the guard also
  accepts; h2, h4 and h6 agree to 0.0005 c.

THE REMEDY IS CAPPED AT THE HARMONIC REQUESTED, and that is not cosmetic.
Uncapped, the highest partial above -40 dB at note 36 is h132. Recommending it
would walk straight into the band-limit failure estimate_f0_harmonic already
documents (h64 at note 36: sd 0.14 c with the mean 57 c wrong). Having energy
is necessary, not sufficient, and a guard that hands you the next defect is
worse than no guard.
