=== WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SAY ===

DOES: the claim's 63:39:0 proportionality is refuted, and the AFFINE law I
proposed from three rows is refuted too, by the fourth row it predicted at
+2.0661 c against a measured +1.7122. I published that affine law in 10687d4
with the sentence "three points and two parameters is a weak test of a
two-parameter law; a fourth note would test it properly". It did, and it
failed. Recorded here rather than quietly replaced.

DOES: the mechanism's SHAPE survives. The shift is monotone decreasing in cv
across four notes spanning the full DAC range, largest at cv 0. What fails is
every simple law anyone has written for it.

DOES NOT: say what the law is. A quadratic is not proposed as physics; it is
in the table only to show that adding a free parameter does not rescue the fit
(chi2 25.3 on 1 dof).

THE OUTLIER IS NOTE 60, IN EVERY FIT, always high (+3.7, +4.8, +4.5 sd). Two
readings, and they are distinguishable by measurement rather than by taste:

  (a) one bad repeat. n60-r2 is +3.2363 against +2.7401 +2.8729 +2.9468
      +2.7347, which is 4.0 sd from the other four. NOTHING in its own
      diagnostics flags it: its cell sds (0.5209, 0.3675) sit among its
      neighbours', and the screen dropped nothing from it. So it cannot be
      removed on any principled ground, and removing it because it is
      inconvenient is the failure this claim has already had once.
  (b) the law is genuinely not smooth in cv. A 6-bit DAC's differential
      nonlinearity is code-dependent, and a rail shift read through it would
      not be a smooth function of the code. That would make "the law" the
      wrong question and per-code data the right one, which is a G13 result
      rather than a defeat.

is written. If (a), the pooled row falls toward +2.82 and the fits improve. If
(b), it holds near +2.91 and the next job is a cv sweep rather than a law.
